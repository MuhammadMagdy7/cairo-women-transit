# Synthetic data — method and assumptions

The GTFS feed describes **supply only** (routes, stops, timetables). Riders, rides and
safety reports do not exist as open data, so `app.*` is **synthetic**, generated by
`python/03_generate_synthetic.py` (seed 42, reproducible). Every row in
`app.safety_reports` carries `source = 'synthetic'`.

## What is tied to the real network
- Ride demand per route and time window ∝ seats offered = vehicles in window
  (from `frequencies.txt`) × vehicle capacity × period demand factor.
- Board/alight stops are real stops on that route, in travel order.
- Wait time ~ exponential with mean = half the real headway.
- 65 % of a rider's trips use routes serving her home stop.
- Busy interchange stops (served by many routes) get a hotspot risk factor 1.0–1.6.

## Assumptions (stated, not observed)
| item | value |
|---|---|
| vehicle seats | microbus 14, Suzuki 8, coop 29, CTA bus 60, minibus 30, Peugeot 7 |
| crowding risk | CTA bus 1.45, CTA minibus 1.30, microbus 1.25, coop 1.15, Suzuki 0.85 |
| period demand / risk | AM peak 1.40/1.60, Midday 0.90/1.00, PM peak 1.30/1.50, Evening 0.60/1.35 |
| weekend | Friday demand 0.5, Saturday 0.7; risk × 0.8 |
| long waits | risk × (1 + wait_min / 60) |
| journey stage mix | on board 33 %, waiting 28 %, boarding 17 %, walk to stop 15 %, alighting 7 % |
| fares | assumed base fare per operator × 0.9–1.4 |

## Calibration
Base incident probability was tuned so that ~80 % of riders report at least one
incident during 2025, matching the ITDP / UN-Habitat Greater Cairo study finding that
over 80 % of women surveyed face harassment at some stage of their journey.

Result (seed 42): 4,000 riders · 192,692 rides · 6,763 reports ·
81.7 % riders with ≥ 1 incident · 35 reports per 1,000 rides.

## Limitations
Findings about *which* routes are riskiest reflect these assumptions plus real network
structure — they show the method, not measured reality. Replace or blend with survey or
HarassMap data (`source` column) to make claims about real conditions.
